# 29.3 - IAM PassRole

## What is PassRole?

When you assign an IAM role to an AWS service (EC2, Lambda, CodePipeline…), you are **passing** that role to the service. To do this, you need the `iam:PassRole` permission in your own IAM policy.

---

## How it works

```
Developer (your IAM user)
        │
        │  needs iam:PassRole permission
        ▼
  Assigns role → EC2 Instance / Lambda / CodePipeline
        │
        │  Service assumes the role via trust policy
        ▼
  Service can act with the role's permissions
```

---

## Required permissions

To pass a role to a service, you need TWO permissions:

| Permission | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `iam:PassRole` | Allows you to assign the role to the service |
| `iam:GetRole` | Allows you to view the role being passed (often paired) |

### Example IAM policy

```json
{
  "Effect": "Allow",
  "Action": ["ec2:*"],
  "Resource": "*"
},
{
  "Effect": "Allow",
  "Action": ["iam:PassRole"],
  "Resource": "arn:aws:iam::123456789:role/S3Access"
}
```

This policy allows the user to do anything on EC2 **but only pass the `S3Access` role** — no other role can be assigned.

---

## Trust Policy — Can any role be passed to any service?

**No.** A role can only be passed to a service if that service is listed in the role's **trust policy**.

### Example trust policy (allows only EC2 to assume this role)

```json
{
  "Effect": "Allow",
  "Principal": {
    "Service": "ec2.amazonaws.com"
  },
  "Action": "sts:AssumeRole"
}
```

This role cannot be passed to Lambda or CodePipeline — only EC2.

---

## Exam Tips

- ✅ Passing a role to a service requires `iam:PassRole` on your user policy
- ✅ `iam:GetRole` is often paired with `iam:PassRole`
- ✅ Roles can **only** be passed to services listed in their **trust policy**
- ✅ Trust policy = "which services can assume this role"
- ✅ You've used this throughout the course every time you assigned a role to EC2/Lambda/CodePipeline
